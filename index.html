What is Plug & Play Solar?

Plug-in solar, sometimes called balcony solar or balcony PV, provides households with a small solar generation system designed primarily for household self-consumption.

1

Generate

One or two solar panels convert daylight into renewable DC electricity.

2

Convert

A compatible microinverter converts the electricity into AC electricity suitable for household use.

3

Use

Electricity generated during daylight can reduce electricity imported from the grid.

Plug-in Solar in Ireland

Ireland is developing a dedicated regulatory pathway for small plug-in solar systems.

Current status - November 2026

Ireland's simplified plug-and-play framework is not yet operational. Customers should not assume that a European plug-in solar kit can currently be connected directly to an Irish household socket.

The developing Irish approach is centred on compliant systems with a maximum output of approximately 800W AC, together with appropriate product standards and registration requirements.

What's expected in 2027?

Ireland is working towards a simplified pathway under which a compliant plug-in solar device could be connected to a suitable existing socket without requiring installation by a certified electrician.

The opening of the Irish plug-in solar market is currently targeted during 2027, subject to completion of the necessary legislation, safety requirements, standards and network arrangements.

Plug & Play Solar will only supply systems for grid-connected plug-in use in Ireland when that use is permitted and the relevant equipment requirements have been confirmed.

Regulatory information last reviewed: November 2026. Regulations can change. Customers should check the applicable requirements before purchasing or connecting generation equipment.

What Could an 800W Solar Kit Produce?

A well-positioned 800Wp solar panel array in Ireland could typically generate approximately 650-800 kWh of electricity per year. Actual output depends on location, orientation, shading, panel angle, inverter efficiency and other site conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is plug-in solar currently legal in Ireland?

Ireland does not yet have the simplified plug-and-play framework envisaged for 2027. Consumers should wait for confirmation of the final Irish rules before connecting a plug-in generating system to a household socket.

When will Plug & Play Solar kits become available?

We intend to supply suitable systems once Ireland's regulatory framework permits their use and the relevant technical and product requirements have been confirmed.

Will I need an electrician?

The proposed simplified framework is intended to make compliant small systems easier to connect. The final Irish connection and safety requirements must be confirmed.

How much electricity could an 800W system generate?

Approximately 650-800 kWh per year can be a reasonable indicative range for a well-positioned 800Wp installation in Ireland.

Does solar work during the Irish winter?

Yes. Solar panels generate electricity from daylight rather than heat, although winter generation is substantially lower than summer generation.

Will the solar panels work during a power cut?

A conventional grid-connected microinverter normally shuts down automatically when the electricity network fails. This anti-islanding protection is an important safety measure.

Will I be paid for electricity exported to the grid?

The treatment of exported electricity under Ireland's future plug-in framework should be checked once final arrangements are published. The financial case should primarily be based on electricity used directly within the home.

Does Plug & Play Solar install the system?

No. Plug & Play Solar is intended to operate as a supply-only business, providing compatible solar kits once those systems can legally be supplied for their intended use in Ireland.
